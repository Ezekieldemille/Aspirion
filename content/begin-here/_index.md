<h3 class="bh-group-heading">Understanding Reality</h3>
<details class="bh-entry">
<summary>On Reality</summary>
<div class="bh-answer"><p>Reality can be broadly separated into three categories; Fundamental Reality, Pseudo-Objective reality, and Intersubjective reality. Examples include atoms, forces, and molecules (fundamental reality), stones, trees, and rivers (pseudo-objective reality), and nations, languages, and money (intersubjective reality). Importantly, Aspirion holds that "gods" exist on the intersubjective layer of reality.</p><p class="bh-pending">Full article forthcoming.</p></div>
</details>
<details class="bh-entry">
<summary>On Aspirils</summary>
<div class="bh-answer"><p>An <em>Aspiril</em> is an object of worship that informs the direction of a person's development. "Gods" are a specific type of Aspiril, though they do not exhaust the category. Aspirils come in two primary forms, personal Aspirils and Impersonal or natural Aspirils. A personal Aspiril is a being you can conceivably become more literally similar to. A Natural Aspiril a being you cannot conceivably become more literally like, but rather, seek to develop yourself because of or towards. </p><p class="bh-pending">Full article forthcoming.</p></div>
</details>

<h2 id="bh-faq-heading">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

<h3 class="bh-group-heading">Identity</h3>
<details class="bh-entry">
<summary>What is Aspirion?</summary>
<div class="bh-answer"><p>Aspirion is a religious tradition focused on maintaining the spiritual technology of religion, without resorting to super natural justifications for the maintenance thereof. It is best understood as a nonsupernaturalist religion.</p><p>Aspirion is a living religious tradition, with room for many opinions and philosophies. It advocates only that one's worship and self-development are intentionally and productively perused.</p></div>
</details>
<details class="bh-entry">
<summary>Is Aspirion a religion?</summary>
<div class="bh-answer"><p>Yes. Aspirion deliberately engages in religious practices such as worship, prayer, and altar practice. It is different from many other religions in that it is nonsupernaturalist, and doesn't claim that it is engaging with any divine beings who have the power to grant magical boons or answer prayers with miracles</p><p>The Aspirite approach to life is modeled around religion as a tool that can both direct one's self-development and also the development of one's community, and by extension the entire world</p></div>
</details>
<details class="bh-entry">
<summary>Is Aspirion atheistic?</summary>
<div class="bh-answer"><p>This depends, like so many things, on the definitions accepted for the conversation. If, by <em>atheistic</em> you mean that Aspirites reject the existence of God/gods unilaterally, then no, we are not atheistic. However, if by <em>atheistic</em> you mean <em>one who rejects the existence of a literal supernatural being who exists in a spiritual realm separate from and outside of the human experience<em>, then yes, Aspirion is an atheistic religion. </p><p>Aspirion affirms that gods exist as a particular category of <em>Aspiril<em>: being existing purely within the intersubjective layer of reality, and regularly effecting how people live, what they value, and how they direct their time and resources. Aspirion rejects only the supernatural claim that gods exist on the same layer of reality as, say, stones or trees. We have no evidence to support the notion that such  being exists, despite immense evidence that the gods - as Aspirils - exist in much the same way as nations, money, languages, or the meanings of songs exist.</p></div>
</details>
<h3 class="bh-group-heading">Gods and Reality</h3>
<details class="bh-entry">
<summary>Does Aspirion believe in God?</summary>
<div class="bh-answer"><p><strong>God is unequivocally real</strong>.Capital-G God, in nations where Abrahamic faiths are dominant, is a particular Aspiril that represents the way a moral life ought to be lived, including such attributes as emotional control, righteous judgement, and reciprocal moral focus.</p><p>God influences people and nations through their beliefs, their traditions, their institutions, and their choices.</p></div>
</details>
<details class="bh-entry">
<summary>How can a god be real without supernatural powers?</summary>
<div class="bh-answer"><p>French is real. You would have to be crazy to suggest that just because you can't measure out a cup of French, or tell me how many atoms of French exist in a square meter of France. Similarly, a drawing of a fish is real, despite the fact that it isn't a fish and no part of the painting has anything to do with a fish on its own; yet the entire construct of the painting is a reflection of the idea of a fish as it existed within the mind of the artist. </p><p>Gods are real in much the same way as images of real things and as languages.</p> In many ways gods are reflections of ideals important to the people that believe in them or the cultures that generated them as a whole. Acknowledgment of the reality of Aspirils </div>
</details>
<details class="bh-entry">
<summary>Does Aspirion believe in objective reality?</summary>
<div class="bh-answer"><p>Yes we do! Objective reality is what Aspirion refers to internally as fundamental reality. Aspirion also believes that humans are not independently well evolved to understand objective reality. e are, at our core, Niche-Switching-Pattern-Exploiters. This is what our senses and instincts are geared around. This often overlaps with an ability to understand elements of fundamental reality, however, it is not universal or even particularly expansive. But! By cross-referencing the experiences of many people and replicating experiments under very controlled conditions we CAN start to uncover elements of fundamental reality out of many people's subjective experiments. So, it is real...but rarely obvious.</p></div>
</details>
<details class="bh-entry">
<summary>Isn't all this just subjective?</summary>
<div class="bh-answer"><p>Some parts are, some parts aren't. Calling the things we orient our lives around Aspirils, or how we divide up reality, or the words we use, or the metaphors we find meaningful...yes, all of these are inherently subjective. But being subjective doesn't mean it is unreal, or that it isn't meaningful. In many ways, these things we talk about are intersubjective, meaning that they are not beholden to only one person's whims, but rather, a whole community holds them together. Parts, however, remain demonstrable and resonant outside of any single individuals experience, and so it would not be accurate to describe every part of the philosophy as ''just subjective.''</p></div>
</details>
<details class="bh-entry">
<summary>What does it mean to externalize moral authority to God?</summary>
<div class="bh-answer"><p>A key concern that motivate the establishment of Aspirion was the observation that many people following diverse religions have a tendency to externalize their moral locus. This is the process of not considering whether something is right or wrong, but instead considering whether or not an outside personality would approve or disapprove of it. More often than not in the Sphere of the European Diaspora this means ''God.'' The issue with this is, of course, that this external locus is mediated not by an actual Divine Law Giver who, sitting on his golden throne, can see into the eternities and has so superior a cognition to us that he can justify any horror against an assured future benefit. Rather, as has been our sad experience time and time again, we find that, even within traditions claiming unique access to the divine, this locus ultimately find itself in either the mind of philosophy of another mortal. And so far they have more often left society wanting for wisdom than been some great source. This is not inherently bad. For instance, if you are yourself not an automotive engineer, you must externalize some form of locus to an engineer that either designed or cares for your vehicle. This is reasonable in some times and places and contexts. However, when it is in particular the moral locus we discuss...it is exceptionally dangerous to externalize that. We ought to keep our sense of right and wrong internal, and judge for ourselves right and wrong, rather than examining the approval or disapproval of another being, however wise they may seem to us in that moment. </p></div>
</details>
<h3 class="bh-group-heading">Practice and Participation</h3>
<details class="bh-entry">
<summary>What do Aspirionists worship?</summary>
<div class="bh-answer"><p>Aspirion uses the term <em>aspiril</em> for an object of worship that informs the direction of a person's development. An aspiril need not be a supernatural being.</p><p>That makes the question of worship practical as well as philosophical: what do we orient ourselves toward, and what are we becoming through that orientation?</p></div>
</details>
<details class="bh-entry">
<summary>Why use prayer and altars in a nonsupernatural religion?</summary>
<div class="bh-answer"><p>Religious practices can be meaningful because of what they do in human lives, regardless of whether a supernatural being acts in response to them.</p><p>Aspirion includes prayer and altar practice among the subjects of its practical teaching. The more detailed explanations of those practices belong in their respective articles.</p></div>
</details>
<details class="bh-entry">
<summary>Where should I start?</summary>
<div class="bh-answer"><p>Start with the irreducible virtues, then consider the three paths. The articles on reality and aspirils establish the philosophical background for Aspirion's religious vocabulary, while the articles on worship, prayer, and altars explore practical expression.</p><p>You can also begin with whichever question on this page matters most to you. There is no need to pretend to understand or agree with something you have not yet examined.</p></div>
</details>
