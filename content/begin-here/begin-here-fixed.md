<p class="bh-eyebrow">QUESTIONS FROM THE OUTSIDE</p>
<h2 id="bh-faq-heading">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p>Curious, skeptical, or just getting acquainted? Start with the question that brought you here.</p>
</div>
<h3 class="bh-group-heading">Identity</h3>
<details class="bh-entry">
<summary>What is Aspirion?</summary>
<div class="bh-answer"><p>Aspirion is a nonsupernatural religion that uses religious language, reflection, and practice to support human development and community building. Its three irreducible virtues are Truth, Struggle, and Becoming; its three paths are Self-Discovery, Self-Development, and Community Building.</p><p>Aspirion is presented as a living philosophy to examine and practice, not a claim that every question about reality has already been settled.</p></div>
</details>
<details class="bh-entry">
<summary>Is Aspirion a religion?</summary>
<div class="bh-answer"><p>Yes. Aspirion deliberately engages in religious practices such as worship, prayer, and altar practice, while rejecting unsupported claims of supernatural direction or power.</p><p>Its approach treats religion as something human beings can use to shape their development and their communities.</p></div>
</details>
<details class="bh-entry">
<summary>Is Aspirion atheistic?</summary>
<div class="bh-answer"><p>That depends partly on what someone means by <em>atheistic</em>. Aspirion rejects the claim that a supernatural higher power guides human affairs, because the evidence does not support that claim.</p><p>At the same time, Aspirion affirms that gods exist as aspirils: real patterns of meaning, worship, and influence in human lives. Rejecting supernatural attributes does not require denying the reality of those patterns.</p></div>
</details>
<h3 class="bh-group-heading">Gods and Reality</h3>
<details class="bh-entry">
<summary>Does Aspirion believe in God?</summary>
<div class="bh-answer"><p><strong>God is real</strong> in Aspirion's understanding, but not as a physical object or independently existing supernatural being. Capital-G God names a particular aspiril associated with Abrahamic understandings of moral living and righteous judgment.</p><p>God influences people and nations through beliefs, traditions, institutions, and choices. That influence does not establish an ability to perform supernatural acts or supply independent, infallible moral judgments.</p></div>
</details>
<details class="bh-entry">
<summary>How can a god be real without supernatural powers?</summary>
<div class="bh-answer"><p>A drawing of a fish is real, even though it is not a fish. A language, a nation, and a social contract are real, even though none is a single object that can be measured out by the cup.</p><p>Likewise, gods are real as meaningful patterns that influence human thought and conduct. The existence of a pattern does not prove that every story told about it is literally true.</p></div>
</details>
<details class="bh-entry">
<summary>Does Aspirion believe in objective reality?</summary>
<div class="bh-answer"><p>Yes. Reality does not depend on human agreement for its existence. But our understandings of it are representations, not the underlying reality itself.</p><p>Because our minds recognize patterns and can misinterpret them, Aspirion values comparing perspectives, testing claims, and identifying patterns that withstand scrutiny. Agreement is not proof, but careful intersubjective examination can bring us closer to what is true.</p></div>
</details>
<details class="bh-entry">
<summary>Isn't all this just subjective?</summary>
<div class="bh-answer"><p>No. The fact that a representation depends on minds does not make that representation unreal or its consequences imaginary. A law, language, promise, or social contract can shape what people actually do.</p><p>At the same time, the usefulness or popularity of an interpretation does not guarantee that it accurately describes the world beyond those human practices. Aspirion distinguishes the reality of an idea from the accuracy of claims about its referent.</p></div>
</details>
<details class="bh-entry">
<summary>What does it mean to externalize moral authority to God?</summary>
<div class="bh-answer"><p>People may frame moral questions as questions about what God requires, placing the apparent source of judgment beyond themselves. This can create shared expectations, encourage responsibility, and support cooperation.</p><p>It can also make human interpretations seem unquestionable or allow people to transfer responsibility for their choices to an alleged divine command. Aspirion recognizes both the strengths and the risks of this social function.</p></div>
</details>
<h3 class="bh-group-heading">Practice and Participation</h3>
<details class="bh-entry">
<summary>What do Aspirionists worship?</summary>
<div class="bh-answer"><p>Aspirion uses the term <em>aspiril</em> for an object of worship that informs the direction of a person's development. An aspiril need not be a supernatural being.</p><p>That makes the question of worship practical as well as philosophical: what do we orient ourselves toward, and what are we becoming through that orientation?</p></div>
</details>
<details class="bh-entry">
<summary>Why use prayer and altars in a nonsupernatural religion?</summary>
<div class="bh-answer"><p>Religious practices can be meaningful because of what they do in human lives, regardless of whether a supernatural being acts in response to them.</p><p>Aspirion includes prayer and altar practice among the subjects of its practical teaching. The more detailed explanations of those practices belong in their respective articles.</p></div>
</details>
<details class="bh-entry">
<summary>Where should I start?</summary>
<div class="bh-answer"><p>Start with the irreducible virtues, then consider the three paths. The articles on reality and aspirils establish the philosophical background for Aspirion's religious vocabulary, while the articles on worship, prayer, and altars explore practical expression.</p><p>You can also begin with whichever question on this page matters most to you. There is no need to pretend to understand or agree with something you have not yet examined.</p></div>
</details>
